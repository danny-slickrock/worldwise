# ADR 0004 — Globe level of detail: sharper boundaries as you zoom

**Status:** Proposed. Plan only; no code until reviewed.
**Date:** 2026-11-04
**Related:** M2.3.7 (the globe), `scripts/build-worldmap.mjs`, `src/data/worldMap.js`,
`src/data/worldGeo.js`, `src/game/globeProjection.js`, `src/components/GlobeMap.js`,
the 2026-11 deep-zoom change (`GLOBE_ZOOM_MAX` 4 → 32)

## Context

Every globe now zooms to 32×, roughly a 4° field of view. The geometry hasn't kept up. Every
border and coastline comes from **Natural Earth 1:110m**, chosen when the globe stopped at 4×.
At 110m a coastline vertex is typically 20–60 km from the next. At 32× on a phone that is
tens of screen pixels per segment, so coasts read as polygons drawn with a ruler, and the
smallest countries have no real shape at all: the 29 micro-states are point symbols.

Measured today (desktop V8, `test/` harness, 2026-11-04):

| | 110m (shipped) |
|---|---|
| Bundle size (`worldMap.js`) | 110 KB |
| Countries with rings | 204 |
| Rings / points | 294 / **9,398** |
| Project every country, one frame (`projectCountry`, path strings) | **1.32 ms** |

A phone runs this JavaScript about 2.5× slower (the ratio measured for the raster work in
the motion-sharpness change), so about 3.3 ms. That shares a 16.7 ms frame with the terrain
raster (budgeted at ~8 ms) and the browser's own SVG paint.

## Decision

Three geometry tiers, swapped by zoom, refining **only what is on screen**.

### Tiers and thresholds

| Tier | Source | Zoom band | Field of view at the band's floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | NE 110m (unchanged, still bundled) | 1 – 3 | whole hemisphere to ~19° |
| L1 | NE 50m | 3 – 10 | ~19° to ~6° |
| L2 | NE 10m | ≥ 10 | ≤ ~6° |

The thresholds put a tier's typical vertex spacing at a few screen pixels at the band's
floor. They are **constants** (`GLOBE_LOD_*`), and are retuned from the measurements in
phase 1, not from these estimates. Swaps use **hysteresis** (e.g. enter L1 at 3.0×, leave
it below 2.6×) so a pinch hovering at a boundary can't flicker between tiers.

### Estimated data sizes (to be replaced by measured numbers in phase 1)

| Tier | Points (est.) | Raw GeoJSON (est.) | Shipped as (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 110m | 9.4 k (measured) | — | 110 KB, bundled (as today) |
| L1 50m | ~100 k | ~4 MB | ~1 MB quantized; **fetched**, not bundled |
| L2 10m | ~550 k+ | ~25 MB | ~6–10 MB quantized, **per-country chunks**, fetched on demand |

Nothing past L0 enters the JS bundle: the convention is runtime data over large embedded
assets, and 10m alone would multiply the app's download size. Detail is generated by
`build-worldmap.mjs`, stored as per-country, delta-encoded, quantized integer arrays (Int32;
1e-5° ≈ 1 m precision is far finer than any zoom here), and served as static assets with
the web build. A country is fetched the first time its tier is needed, held in memory, and
cached on device so it isn't refetched.

Each tier is **simplified at build time** (Visvalingam, or Douglas–Peucker as fallback) to a
tolerance of about half a screen pixel at that tier's floor zoom. That throws away vertices
nobody could see, and is what keeps L2 from being unaffordable.

### Per-frame budget: it must stay smooth on a phone

**The rule: at most ~20,000 projected points per frame, on any tier, at any zoom.** That is
about 2× today's L0 cost, so about 7 ms on a mid-range phone. Three mechanisms keep it there:

1. **Viewport culling.** At zoom z the visible cap has angular radius ≈ asin(1/z): about 19°
   at 3×, 6° at 10× and 1.8° at 32×. Only countries whose bounding cap intersects it are
   projected at the detailed tier. Everything else is culled (`projectRing` already drops the
   back face). Deep zoom shows a handful of countries, so detail is spent where it shows.
2. **A coarse tier while moving, the fine tier when settled.** The same pattern the terrain
   raster uses. During a drag, pinch or momentum coast, the globe draws one tier coarser;
   about 160 ms after it settles, it swaps to the target tier. Detail is invisible on a
   moving globe, and frame drops are not.
3. **A hard ceiling.** If a frame's visible set would exceed the budget (a wide view at the
   bottom of a band, say), it falls back to the coarser tier for the outer countries and
   keeps detail for the one under the centre.

A test pins the budget against fixture geometry; device verification happens in phase 4,
not in desktop Chromium alone.

### Correctness rules to keep

- **Gameplay is untouched.** `COUNTRY_PATHS`' keys (L0) still decide what the Country
  Locator can ask about. LOD is presentation only; a micro-state that gains a real L2 shape
  stays explorable, not askable.
- **Same projection everywhere.** All tiers go through `globeProjection.js`, so borders stay
  registered with the terrain raster by construction.
- **Neighbours share a tier.** Within one view, every visible country is drawn at the same
  tier. Mixing 110m Italy with 10m Switzerland would open slivers along the shared border.
- **Somaliland / Northern Cyprus merges** (into `so` / `cy`) and the territories registry are
  re-applied per tier by the generator, exactly as for 110m, or two holes reopen.
- **Hit targets** keep their current small-country enlargement at L0, and use real L2
  outlines where they exist.

### Out of scope

- **Terrain sharpness at depth.** The 4096 × 2048 basemap is ~0.09° per texel. At 32× it is
  upscaled about 2–3×, so imagery stays soft even with crisp borders. Regional high-res
  imagery tiles would be a separate ADR.
- **Native.** iOS and Android draw the same SVG paths, so the plan applies, but phase 4 must
  verify on a real device. That's still the open item from M2.3.7 step 3.

## Phases (each its own commit, after this ADR is approved)

1. **Generator + measurement.** `build-worldmap.mjs` emits L1/L2 from NE 50m/10m (public
   domain), simplified and quantized per country. Record the real point counts and sizes in
   this ADR and retune the thresholds.
2. **Loader + cache.** Fetch-on-demand per country and tier, with an in-memory map and
   on-device cache. Pure decode (delta + quantization) lives in `game/` and is tested.
3. **LOD selection in `GlobeMap`.** A pure `lodFor(zoom, moving, previousTier)` with
   hysteresis, viewport culling and the point ceiling. Coarse while moving, refine on settle.
4. **Verification.** Frame-time measurements under CPU throttling and on a real phone, plus
   screenshots at 3×, 10× and 32× of the Alps, the Caribbean and Singapore.

## Consequences

- Deep zoom becomes useful for the small countries it was raised for.
- The app gains a runtime dependency on static geometry assets for detail. Offline, or
  before a fetch lands, a view simply stays at L0. That's the same graceful floor as today.
- The bundle stays the same size; download cost is paid only by people who zoom.
